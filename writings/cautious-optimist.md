Dear Mr. President,

The reception from your supporters at Kasarani was something to behold. The crowds were electric, and the energy was unmistakable. Now some would scoff at that and say that the bar for my amazement is very low. "It is the usual," they would say. In fact, it is to be expected given the feverish nature of Kenyan politics and the circumstances around the electoral season that had a hand in upping the ante and raising the stakes around what would be the eventual announcement of the president elect. The excitement at Kasarani was nothing more than the culmination of a perfect storm that had nothing to do with the president elect and all to do with the circumstances around his ascendancy. Similar to a last minute goal in a Champions League final, the emotional high was the result of the release of the pent up anticipation. Such lengthy hypothesizing is however the reserve of the sore looser who would do everything to deny his rival credit.

My amazement, contrary to the naysayer was not just at the joy and the raw emotions displayed by your supporters Mr. President, but rather how heartfelt it felt and how palpable the optimism was. This wasn't just the raw emotions associated with getting one up on the other side, there was a belief among the masses present at Kasarani that indeed a new dawn had arrived. Freedom was here.

The conscientious analyst would then have to question why this was. Why this belief. The skeptic would be quick to say that this was simply the result of what had been a systematic cultivation of disdain for the opposition who represented the establishment you so firmly, and in some instances rightly decried. The hustler-dynasty paradigm you taught as gospel was readily imbibed by those who needed a scapegoat for their frustrations. The believer on the other hand would argue contrary to the skeptic, that this belief was not just a simple denouncement of the establishment born out of spite, but rather a demonstration of faith in the agenda you placed before the people.

I find myself somewhere in the middle of the road. It is clear that some of your supporters became converts to spite your opposition. The actions of a rebellious teenager. However, optimism is not born out of spite, there are those among your supporters who with every fiber of their being, believe that you wish the best for them. I tend more to the latter. I believe you, Mr. President won the people over through the power of narrative. A narrative your critics call carefully concocted, stating that the faith you have inspired is misguided, given your alleged checkered past. They have in similar terms, and worse, called you a wolf in sheep's clothing. I myself I'm unconvinced as to the verity of all the claims, what with the suspect nature of the constant public assailment of your character. However, I would be unwise not to approach with caution. Where there is smoke, there is fire, the penchant for political pyromania between political opponents notwithstanding. Such however, is the unfortunate state of our politics where your checkered past is in no way the exception and gentlemanly conduct remains a mirage. Therefore, for now I must withhold my criticisms.

I find myself among the masses you have wooed. The masses who the self-proclaimed intellectual superiors seem to be brimming with disdain for. They claim that we have been hoodwinked because of our incurable ignorance, forgetting that even the best of men can be manipulated. Especially those with delusions of self-grandeur. Therefore, though susceptible to manipulation, I do not believe the masses to be incapable of reason. I do not believe that they have trusted you without knowledge of all your alleged misdeeds, I believe that they have trusted you in spite of them, and I find myself a cautious convert. I refuse to believe that a man would simply turn his back on the faith demonstrated to him by the millions of Kenyans whose support to him has been so sincere. I have been cautioned before that this is where my naivete and misguided romanticism shows, "Rod," say they, "this is the story of politics; bait, switch, repeat." The public refuses to learn and so remains the status quo.

Indeed, some would say that your message even found fertile soil, that you preyed on the sense of neglect felt by those who were antagonized by a state they felt didn't care for them. In the same breath however, you overcame the growing political apathy among those who believed that no politician could change the status quo. Perhaps your background gave them reason to believe, perhaps your posturing as a hero to the oppressed hoodwinked them, perhaps your logic and ideas impressed, convinced and convicted some. Whatever it was, you struck a chord with large swaths of the Kenyan populace, and for that, I am loathe to believe that your claims were simply a facade and that the massive support was nothing more than religious, intellectual and tribal ignorance as some have claimed. There surely must be more than meets the eye with this man, and as a firm believer in redemption, I want to be proven right.

Mr. President, it doesn't escape me that your detractors had reason to doubt. It is your duty to address these doubts. For better for worse, ours is a winner take all society, and so the heart of all of Kenya is in your hand. Whether by fate, cunning or providence, surely you cannot miss the gravity of the moment brought upon you. Surely you will not validate the doubts that lead to apathy and smash to pieces the very dreams and aspiration that have lit your path to the presidency. If you do, I fail to fathom the depths of despair that would hang over this nation and the political indifference that would result therefrom. Quite dramatic, but I really do believe this to be a watershed moment in Kenyan politics. Mr. President, you are no Messiah and I do not expect you to lead us into all truth much less turn water into wine. However, if you are indeed true to your promises of an independent judiciary, a financially autonomous police force, a free, fair and open democracy, a more or less deregulated economy, among your other promises, you stand to be a luminary.

I do not expect instant results, and I know these first few months will be full of undue criticism from those who expect the same. Rest assured, my voice will not be among those, I shall be among those wishing you Godspeed, the wisdom to steward the hope you have inspired among many and the faith to assuage the doubts you yourself may have. Prove my cautiousness unfounded, prove your detractors wrong, prove that people are not wrong to have hope that things and people change. Regardless of your performance, I will keep believing in change and in a better Kenya and I hope so too will everyone who reads this. That's how change happens. As for me, my ultimate hope is not you Sir, but the God you claim to serve.
